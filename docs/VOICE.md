# Woodland Grain voice

Woodland Grain Works should sound like a capable person in a well-equipped shop, not a design agency, a large team, or a polished brand generator.

## Point of view

- Use **I** when the workshop speaks about its own work or process.
- Use **you** when speaking to the visitor.
- Use **we** only when the sentence genuinely describes working through a project together.
- Use **Woodland Grain Works** for formal descriptions, metadata, and places where the business name matters.

## Tone

- Direct, practical, curious, and quietly confident.
- Professional without sounding corporate.
- Personal without becoming overly casual.
- Specific about materials, tools, fit, limits, and use.
- Honest about concept studies, current capabilities, and missing contact infrastructure.

Short lines with a little personality are welcome: “odd problems welcome,” “start with what you know,” and “not knowing yet is fine.” The character should come from an observant maker’s point of view, not fantasy language or advertising hype.

## Avoid

- Agency language such as “we deliver solutions,” “visual language,” “cross-disciplinary innovation,” or “industry-leading.”
- Empty craft language such as “passion,” “journey,” “bespoke excellence,” or “where imagination comes to life.”
- Claims about pricing, turnaround, equipment, certification, service area, or completed customer work that have not been confirmed.
- Explaining every idea until the copy feels polished flat.

## Copy test

Before publishing a line, ask:

1. Would I say this out loud to someone standing in the shop?
2. Does it tell them something concrete?
3. Does it imply a team, capability, or promise that has not been established?
4. Can it lose a few words and sound more natural?
